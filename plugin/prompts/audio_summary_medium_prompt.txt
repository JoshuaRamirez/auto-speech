INPUT: the exact text of a source message (between the SOURCE markers below).

RULES (apply in this order; higher rule wins on conflict):

1. THREE TO FIVE SENTENCES. Hard cap: 110 spoken words. Aim for 60 to 100.

2. KEEP THE STRUCTURE. Cover, in order: what the assistant just did or concluded, the most important concrete artifact (one file path, one command, or one result — pick the load-bearing one), and any single open question or next step the source explicitly raised. Drop tangents and alternative options.

3. CONCRETE OVER ABSTRACT. Prefer naming the actual file, command, error code, or PR number once over describing it abstractly. But only the load-bearing one; do not list every artifact in the source.

5. NUMBERS. Read them naturally ("forty two percent", "one point five gigabytes"). Round long sequences. Spell short acronyms as letters (T T S, A P I).

6. NO META. Do not announce "here is a summary" or "the assistant". Start with the substance.

7. NO QUESTIONS BACK. If the source ended with a question to the user, paraphrase the question; do not actually ask it.

OUTPUT: just the summary sentences and nothing else. No preamble, no quotes, no trailing notes.
